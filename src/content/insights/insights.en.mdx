# Insights

## The Atrai Bikes Data Platform – What We Built and What We Learned

The atrAI Bikes project has reached an important milestone: a fully functional, open-source geoinformation platform that integrates citizen-generated bicycle sensor data with municipal datasets – and we have validated it in two cities on two continents.

### The Platform

At the core of the system is the **senseBox:bike**, a modular sensor device that records overtaking distances (AI-detected on-device), road vibrations for surface quality assessment, environmental parameters (fine particulate matter, temperature, humidity), and GPS position via a companion mobile app. All data processing happens directly on the device using edge computing, ensuring privacy by design.

The collected data is uploaded to the **openSenseMap** platform and automatically ingested daily into a **PostGIS** database via an OGC API Process. From there, a set of automated analysis pipelines computes road-segment-level indicators:

- **Surface quality** – classifying rough or damaged road sections
- **Overtaking distances** – per road segment, revealing dangerous stretches
- **Danger zones** – combining overtaking proximity and fine dust exposure
- **Traffic flow & speed** – average speeds and flow patterns
- **Campaign statistics** – aggregated summaries per measurement campaign

Results are served as **OGC API Features** via pygeoapi and as vector tiles via the Martin tile server. The interactive web frontend, built on **kepler.gl**, allows users to explore, overlay, and export all datasets. A notable open-source contribution made during the project was adding **OGC WMS support** to kepler.gl, enabling seamless integration of official municipal map layers.

### Validation in Münster and São Paulo

In September 2025, the prototype was evaluated in two workshops with very different audiences:

**Münster** brought together city planners, university researchers, and smart city stakeholders. Participants independently completed three tasks – identifying danger hotspots, comparing temporal variations, and exporting indicators for reports. The feedback highlighted strong practical value, with particular praise for the GIS export functionality and the open, interoperable architecture.

**São Paulo** focused on analytical depth with statistics experts and volunteer data collectors. Discussions around data quality, aggregation methods, and scalability for large-scale measurement campaigns led directly to improvements in direction-aware trip visualization and data aggregation logic.

Both workshops confirmed that **road-segment-level indicators**, derived from cyclists' own perspectives, address a real gap in existing urban mobility data ecosystems – one that commercial platforms (Strava, Komoot) and traditional counting stations cannot fill.

### Key Takeaways

- Citizen-generated sensor data **can** be integrated into municipal GIS workflows using open standards (OGC API)
- The platform is currently at **TRL 5** (technology validated in a relevant environment)
- Participants identified clear demand for better onboarding tutorials, richer metadata documentation, and collaborative annotation features
- Ensuring **data representativeness** remains an open challenge, especially in areas with low cycling activity
- Future work will focus on scalability via vector tiles, on-demand spatial/temporal filters, and broader validation with additional municipalities

The paper presenting these results was published at **FOSSGIS 2026** (DOI: [10.5281/zenodo.18183646](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18183646)).
